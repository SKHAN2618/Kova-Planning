PLANNING EXPLAINED / SMALL DWELLINGS

Can I add a second dwelling?

Understanding the rules for minor dwellings and granny flats.

Reviewed 16 November 2026

THE QUESTION

A second dwelling can create options for family or investment, but the applicable rules depend on the design, site and intended use. New small-dwelling exemptions have specific conditions; they are not automatic permission to build anywhere.

01 / CHECK WHAT IS PROPOSED

A small detached standalone dwelling is not necessarily treated the same way as a house extension, a converted garage or an additional full-sized home. Start with the floor area, height, site position, services and how the dwelling will be used.

02 / SEPARATE THE TWO EXEMPTION TESTS

A qualifying new standalone, single storey dwelling of up to 70 m² may meet the building consent exemption. The national environmental standard provides a separate permitted activity pathway for qualifying detached minor residential units. Passing one set of checks does not mean the other is met.

03 / DESIGN, SERVICES AND NOTICES STILL MATTER

Even exempt building work must comply with the Building Code and relevant conditions. The granny-flat pathway involves licensed professionals, a project information memorandum before work and completion documentation; infrastructure and development contributions may also arise.

04 / CHECK THE SITE BEFORE CHOOSING A PATH

Access, hazards, stormwater, wastewater, service capacity, title constraints and other planning provisions can change what is feasible. Confirm the specific pathway before commissioning final drawings or construction.
