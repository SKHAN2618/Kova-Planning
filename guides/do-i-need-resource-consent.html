Do I actually need resource consent?

How to distinguish a permitted activity from one requiring approval.

Reviewed 16 November 2026

THE QUESTION

Not every change to a property needs resource consent. The question is whether the entire proposal meets the relevant rules and any applicable national direction, not whether similar work has happened next door.

01 / DEFINE THE ACTIVITY

Describe exactly what you want to do: a new building, a changed use, extra earthworks, a second dwelling or something else. Different parts of one proposal can trigger different planning rules.

02 / TEST THE FULL RULE SET

Check the applicable zone, activity classification and development standards, together with overlays, precincts, regional rules and relevant national environmental standards. A permitted activity must meet all applicable conditions for that pathway.

03 / CONSIDER THE OTHER APPROVALS

Even if resource consent is not required, the project may need building consent, specialist design, landowner approvals or checks for existing title restrictions. A planning exemption is not a blanket development approval.

04 / CONFIRM THE DECISION

Record which rules were checked and where uncertainty remains. For some permitted activities a council certificate of compliance can provide formal confirmation; investigate this before relying on informal assumptions.
