PLANNING EXPLAINED / ENVIRONMENTAL EFFECTS

What does an AEE actually explain?

A clear account of a proposal and the effects it may create.

Reviewed 16 November 2026

THE QUESTION

An Assessment of Environmental Effects (AEE) is not simply a formality. It helps explain a proposed activity, what it might change, and how relevant adverse effects will be addressed.

01 / THE PROPOSAL AND ITS CONTEXT

An AEE starts with a clear description of what is proposed and the environment where it would occur. The questions depend on the activity, site conditions and applicable planning framework.

02 / THE EFFECTS

Relevant effects might involve neighbouring amenity, traffic, earthworks, stormwater, landscape, ecological or other matters. The effects considered must be appropriate to the proposal and current statutory requirements.

03 / THE RESPONSE

An assessment identifies appropriate measures to avoid, remedy or mitigate adverse effects and refers to relevant plans and policies. KOVA can coordinate technical evidence from engineers or other specialists where it is needed.

04 / THE RIGHT LEVEL OF DETAIL

The extent of assessment should reflect the proposal and applicable requirements. A short, straightforward proposal does not call for the same evidence as a complex development.
