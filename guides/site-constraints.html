PLANNING EXPLAINED / SITE FEASIBILITY

Why some sites are harder to develop.

The difference between having an idea and knowing whether it can work.

Reviewed 16 November 2026

THE QUESTION

Two properties with similar zoning can have very different development prospects. Planning decisions become more useful when they reflect the real conditions of the site.

01 / LAND AND ACCESS

Slope, lot shape, vehicle access, servicing corridors and existing buildings can affect the scale and layout of a proposal. The site is a physical place, not just an address.

02 / HAZARDS AND INFRASTRUCTURE

Flood hazards, overland flow paths, drainage, wastewater capacity and ground conditions may call for technical input. The availability of services should be checked rather than assumed.

03 / LEGAL AND PLANNING LIMITS

Easements, covenants, consent notices and existing approvals may influence options alongside zoning and other planning rules. Some matters require legal or engineering advice.

04 / PRIORITISE THE UNKNOWN

A useful early review does not promise an approval. It identifies the issues most likely to affect cost, design or timing, and recommends the next investigation before commitments increase.
