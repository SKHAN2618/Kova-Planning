PLANNING EXPLAINED / SITE RULES

Zoning is only the beginning.

Why a zoning label is the first question, not the final answer.

Reviewed 16 November 2026

THE QUESTION

Zoning provides a useful starting point, but it does not tell the full development story. The detail lies in what the proposal triggers and which other controls apply.

01 / START WITH THE ZONE

The zone identifies intended land uses and development outcomes. Its rules may address matters such as building height, setbacks, site coverage and density. But a permitted outcome depends on the whole proposal meeting the relevant provisions.

02 / CHECK OVERLAYS AND OTHER CONTROLS

Overlays, precinct provisions, qualifying matters and regional controls may introduce further restrictions or assessment requirements. A site can look straightforward on a zoning map while being affected by other rules.

03 / CONSIDER THE REAL SITE

Access, topography, stormwater, existing structures and legal interests can influence what can practically be achieved, even when the zone looks favourable.

04 / MAKE AN INFORMED FIRST MOVE

Before investing in detailed designs, review the current planning provisions alongside key physical and title information. Record uncertainties rather than assuming the maximum theoretical yield is achievable.
