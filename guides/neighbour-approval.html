PLANNING EXPLAINED / PEOPLE & EFFECTS

Will my neighbours need to approve?

Affected persons, written approvals and notification explained.

Reviewed 16 November 2026

THE QUESTION

Neighbours are an important part of a place, but not every consent application requires their signature. Whether written approval or notification is relevant depends on the proposal, the current legal tests and how the council assesses effects.

01 / APPROVAL IS NOT AUTOMATIC

A neighbour being nearby does not itself mean written approval is legally necessary. The council determines who may be affected and whether notification is required under the applicable statutory framework.

02 / THE THREE NOTIFICATION PATHS

An application may be non-notified, limited notified to affected parties, or publicly notified. The tests depend on planning rules, national direction, adverse effects that may lawfully be considered and relevant notification provisions.

03 / WHAT WRITTEN APPROVAL MEANS

When a person gives valid written approval, the council generally disregards effects on that person for relevant consent decisions. Those being asked to approve should understand the proposal and its effects; approval should not be treated as a casual signature.

04 / ENGAGE THOUGHTFULLY, NOT AUTOMATICALLY

Good neighbour communication can resolve misunderstandings before they become project disputes. That is different from assuming every proposal requires consultation or that obtaining approval guarantees a consent outcome.
