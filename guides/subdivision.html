Can I subdivide my property?

Why creating another lot involves more than drawing a boundary.

Reviewed 16 November 2026

THE QUESTION

Subdivision begins with an idea about land, but achieving separate titles can involve planning, surveying, infrastructure and legal work. A promising layout is only the start of that pathway.

01 / IS THE SITE SUITABLE?

Check applicable subdivision controls, minimum dimensions where relevant, access, building placement, hazard exposure and the practical service connections for each proposed lot. Zoning alone does not establish subdivisibility.

02 / WHO NEEDS TO BE INVOLVED?

A licensed cadastral surveyor will be needed for the survey and title process. Depending on the site, engineering, transport, drainage, legal and planning specialists may be needed to resolve the proposal and its consent conditions.

03 / FROM CONSENT TO NEW TITLES

Subdivision normally involves a consent pathway, an approved survey plan under section 223 RMA and certification of conditions under section 224(c), followed by registration with Land Information New Zealand. The details depend on the proposal and current legislation.

04 / THE FIRST USEFUL DECISION

A concept layout and feasibility review can establish whether the likely number of lots, access requirements and infrastructure costs justify more detailed survey and design work.
