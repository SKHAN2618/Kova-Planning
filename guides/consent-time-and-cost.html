PLANNING EXPLAINED / PROJECT REALITIES

How long will consent take, and what will it cost?

What affects council processing, fees and project programmes.

Reviewed 16 November 2026

THE QUESTION

Time and cost depend on far more than the application form. A clear early brief can help identify the investigations, design work and council steps likely to shape the programme.

01 / SEPARATE PREPARATION FROM PROCESSING

Before lodgement there may be planning reviews, drawings, specialist reports and consultations. Council statutory processing periods are not the whole project programme and should not be presented as a guaranteed completion date.

02 / KNOW WHAT CAN PAUSE THE CLOCK

Under the current transitional RMA pathway, a standard non-notified consent commonly has a statutory 20-working-day processing timeframe, but information requests, extensions and notification can alter the elapsed time. More complex or notified proposals can take considerably longer.

03 / BUDGET FOR DIFFERENT COST TYPES

A project can involve consultant fees, council deposits and processing charges, technical reports, application revisions and potential development contributions or engineering works. Check the current council fee schedule and ask which charges are fixed, are deposits, or depend on the actual processing work.

04 / REDUCE AVOIDABLE UNCERTAINTY

Check the site early, settle the project scope, identify necessary specialists and lodge an adequately supported application. Even careful preparation cannot guarantee council timing or approval, but it can make the next decisions less speculative.
