PLANNING EXPLAINED / PREPARING AN APPLICATION

What plans and reports will I need?

The drawings, evidence and specialists behind a sound application.

Reviewed 16 November 2026

THE QUESTION

A well-prepared consent application explains the proposal clearly enough for the right questions to be assessed. What is required depends on the site, the activity and the effects that need to be considered.

01 / START WITH ACCURATE SITE INFORMATION

Establish property boundaries, existing buildings, access, title interests, relevant planning provisions and any known hazards. A property file, record of title and appropriate site measurements can avoid design assumptions becoming application problems.

02 / SHOW WHAT WILL CHANGE

Site and floor plans, elevations, earthworks details, servicing layouts or subdivision drawings may be needed. Appropriate design or drafting professionals prepare the drawings; the required level of detail is driven by the proposal and the consent pathway.

03 / EXPLAIN THE PLANNING CASE

Applications under the RMA commonly include an Assessment of Environmental Effects and a planning assessment against relevant provisions. The scope should address matters the law permits the decision-maker to consider and be proportionate to the proposal.

04 / BRING IN SPECIALISTS WHERE THEY ADD VALUE

An engineer, surveyor, transport adviser, arborist, ecologist or other expert may need to provide evidence. KOVA can identify the reports needed and coordinate the specialists who prepare them so the documents support one application.
